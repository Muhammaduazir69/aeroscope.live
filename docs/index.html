AEROSCOPE

What's in your sky — and what deserves a second look.

AeroScope turns crowdsourced ADS-B into a real-time, explainable picture of the aircraft over a chosen point on Earth. It fuses 60+ public feeds, scores each aircraft for attention from transparent rules, and runs a torch-free consensus anomaly detector built entirely on established libraries. It also ships an open, citable benchmark for ADS-B anomaly detection.

THE PROBLEM

Raw ADS-B is a firehose. AeroScope makes it legible.

Every airliner, business jet, helicopter and many drones broadcast their position on 1090 MHz. Dozens of volunteer receiver networks publish that traffic for free — but as undifferentiated dots. The hard part is not collecting positions; it is answering the operator's real questions about a specific patch of sky.

📍

WHO IS UP THERE

Identity resolved from the ICAO 24-bit address, registry, operator and type — including military hex ranges and aircraft broadcasting no callsign.

📈

HOW THEY BEHAVE

Pattern-of-life classification — orbits, racetracks, loiters, grid searches — plus deviation from each airframe's own learned envelope.

🛡️

CAN YOU TRUST THE SIGNAL

DO-260B integrity checks and NIC/NACp/SIL inspection flag implausible or downgraded broadcasts that a naïve tracker would draw as fact.

🎯

WHAT DESERVES ATTENTION

A transparent 0–100 attention score sorts a busy list so the one aircraft worth looking at isn't lost among a hundred routine ones.

CAPABILITIES

A surveillance pipeline, not a map with dots

Each fetch cycle runs raw positions through an enrichment pipeline before anything reaches the screen. Everything below is deployed and running on the live platform.

🌐

MULTI-SOURCE FUSION

adsb.fi, adsb.lol, airplanes.live and OpenSky as terrestrial sources, plus a satellite-ADS-B merge. De-duplicated by ICAO 24-bit address; the same airframe seen by two networks is reconciled, not double-drawn.

🎯

ATTENTION SCORING

Eight weighted rule-based factors — military hex, squawk, envelope anomaly, missing identity, restricted-airspace proximity, pattern, and signal integrity — summed into a 0–100 rank. Always explainable; never a verdict.

8 FACTORS
🧠

CONSENSUS ANOMALY DETECTION

River streaming detection, a scikit-learn IsolationForest baseline, a pykalman trajectory-innovation test, OpenAP envelope checks, stumpy matrix profiles and PyOD. An aircraft is flagged only when at least two detectors agree.

TORCH-FREE
🔍

SPOOFING & INTEGRITY

A DO-260B style 7-check on each broadcast plus a Kalman normalized-innovation (NIS) test. Geometric-vs-barometric altitude, ground-speed-vs-Mach and track-vs-heading residuals expose self-inconsistent messages.

DO-260B
🛸

DRONE / UAV HEURISTICS

Remote-ID parsing combined with behavioural signatures — low-and-slow profiles, tight loiters, non-standard kinematics — surface candidate uncrewed aircraft from the broader picture.

🛰️

REAL-TIME SMOOTH MAP

Client-side dead-reckoning glides every aircraft at its true ground speed between server fixes and corrects toward truth without ever snapping back — continuous motion, not a 30-second teleport.

PATTERN-OF-LIFE

Automatic classification of orbits, racetracks, loiter and search patterns, with per-airframe behavioural baselines (DBSCAN routes, IsolationForest point anomalies) for deviation scoring.

BEHAVIOURAL

ARCHITECTURE

From 1090 MHz to an explainable verdict

A two-tier loop keeps the picture both fresh and deep: a lightweight position refresh keeps markers moving every few seconds, while the heavier enrichment-and-detection pipeline runs on its own cadence so the analysis never blocks the motion.

1

INGEST

60+ crowdsourced ADS-B feeds plus satellite ADS-B, fetched per active map region.

2

FUSE

De-duplicate by ICAO 24-bit address; reconcile terrestrial and satellite views of the same airframe.

3

ENRICH

Registry, Kalman tracks, DO-260B integrity, pattern-of-life and the 8-factor attention score.

4

DETECT

Six independent anomaly detectors vote; a flag fires only on ≥2-detector consensus.

5

SERVE

WebSocket + REST to a browser client that dead-reckons each marker for continuous motion.

The detection layer is deliberately built from established, auditable libraries — River, scikit-learn, pykalman, OpenAP, stumpy, PyOD — rather than an opaque neural network. Every flag can be traced back to the features that produced it. There is no PyTorch and no reinforcement learning in the deployed system; an earlier RL prototype was evaluated and retired in favour of this transparent, reproducible stack.

HOW THE SCORE WORKS

An attention-ranker, shown working

The score exists to sort, not to accuse. It rises when several independent indicators fire at once; the contributing factors are always displayed so an operator can agree or dismiss in a glance.

OPEN RESEARCH

The AeroScope ADS-B Anomaly Benchmark

Anomaly detectors in aviation security are hard to compare because there is little shared, labelled, openly-licensed data. AeroScope publishes a benchmark to help fix that — real traffic, a documented attack taxonomy, and an honest baseline.

WHAT'S IN IT

The benchmark pairs real airborne traffic, captured live from crowdsourced receiver feeds across multiple regions and de-duplicated by ICAO 24-bit address, with synthetically injected attacks following the standard taxonomy in the ADS-B security literature — altitude tampering, velocity tampering, ghost kinematics and integrity-field downgrade (methodology after Habler & Shabtai, Computers & Security, 2018).

Each record carries 38 documented columns: raw ADS-B fields, the signal-integrity fields that matter for spoofing detection (NIC, NACp, NACv, SIL), derived self-consistency features (geometric-vs-barometric altitude difference, ground-speed-vs-Mach residual, barometric-vs-geometric vertical-rate difference, track-vs-heading difference), and the anomaly score from the deployed scikit-learn IsolationForest baseline.

That baseline's measured performance at training time was ROC-AUC ≈ 0.87, with roughly a 50% detection rate at a strict ~2% false-positive rate (threshold calibrated on held-out real traffic).

FAQ

Questions, answered plainly

What exactly is AeroScope?
An observer-relative airspace-intelligence platform. It ingests aircraft positions from 60+ public ADS-B feeds, fuses and de-duplicates them, and presents a real-time, explainable view of the aircraft over a chosen point — their identity, behaviour, signal integrity, and which ones warrant attention. It also publishes an open anomaly-detection benchmark.
Does it use deep learning or reinforcement learning?
No. The anomaly layer is deliberately torch-free and built on established libraries — River, scikit-learn IsolationForest, pykalman, OpenAP, stumpy and PyOD — with consensus voting (≥2 detectors must agree). There is no PyTorch and no reinforcement learning; an earlier RL prototype was retired in favour of this transparent, reproducible stack.
Is the threat score an accusation?
No. It is a 0–100 attention-ranker that sorts a busy list, computed from eight transparent weighted factors. The contributing factors are always shown. It tells an operator where to look first; it does not pass judgement.
What's in the open benchmark, and can I cite it?
38 documented columns pairing real airborne traffic with synthetically injected attacks following the standard ADS-B security taxonomy, including the NIC/NACp/SIL integrity fields and derived self-consistency features. The bundled IsolationForest baseline measured ROC-AUC ≈ 0.87. It is CC-BY 4.0 — cite as "AeroScope ADS-B Anomaly Benchmark v1, aeroscope.live".
Is the data accurate? What are the limits?
Positions are exactly as good as the volunteer ADS-B networks that broadcast them, which is why integrity checking is a first-class feature rather than an afterthought. Documented limitations include synthetic-attack injection as a proxy for real attacks, coverage biased toward busy terminal areas, and sparse enhanced-surveillance fields. These are stated openly in the dataset card.
